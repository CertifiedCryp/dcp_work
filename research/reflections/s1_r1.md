Round 1 — mapping the problem.

1. Exact reformulations under the charged model.
(a) Fourier phase qubits. Applying QFT_N to the second register of |psi_{x,s}> and measuring gives a uniform label k in Z_N and the qubit (|0> + w^{ks}|1>)/sqrt2, w = e^{2 pi i/N}, independent of x. So the mixed state is harmless: rho_s is equivalent to a uniformly random known label plus a phase qubit. Exactness issue: for N not a power of two, QFT_N is not exactly implementable in Clifford+T. The Hales-Hallgren / Kitaev approximate QFT gives a label distribution and post-measurement qubit that are only close (in trace distance) to the ideal ones. Every downstream proof must therefore carry an additive error budget, charged as gate-synthesis cost (Solovay-Kitaev or Ross-Selinger: O(log(1/delta)) T gates per rotation with exact classical cost). This is an important hidden obligation; many papers simply assume an exact QFT.
Alternative that avoids QFT_N: measure the second register in a 2^m-point Fourier basis after padding, with m = n + O(log(1/delta)). This is Kuperberg's "approximate labels" device. Labels then become real numbers k/2^m approximating multiples of 1/N, with phase errors controlled.
(b) CRT. For m | N, mapping the second register x -> x mod m maps rho_s to rho^{(m)}_{s mod m} exactly, because x mod m is uniform. This costs one modular reduction and is exact. Factoring N (Shor) is polynomial but probabilistic; it needs rigorous success and cost accounting, but it is fine. So DCP_N reduces to DCP over prime-power factors plus CRT, with a cost blow-up of at most the number of factors (<= n). Arithmetic structure thus genuinely reduces the problem to N = p^e, and the hard core is N prime (or p^e with large p).
(c) Digit lifting. For N = p^e with s's residue mod p^j known, shifting by the known part and restricting to labels divisible by p^{j}... in the phase picture, a label k = p^{e-j-1} u gives phase w_p^{u s_j}-type information on the next digit. For N = 2^n, label 2^{n-1} reveals the low bit of s exactly (phase +-1), and the Hadamard test is deterministic. This is the "Hensel" structure Kuperberg already exploits: sieve to produce label 2^{n-1}, learn bit 0, then recurse with N/2. For large prime p, a digit lives in Z_p and has no internal structure, so lifting does not help with the prime core.
(d) Hidden shift / subset sum. Regev's reduction relates DCP with failure parameter to unique-SVP. Conversely, combining phase qubits is a subset-sum or k-list problem over Z_N: find signed combinations of labels summing to a target. Kuperberg's sieve is a quantum-guided generalized birthday attack (Wagner). The relevant classical analogue is Wagner's k-tree, which runs in time 2^{O(n/log k)} with k = 2^{sqrt n}, giving the sqrt(n) exponent.

2. Literature with explicit constants (as I recall; this must be verified independently, I cannot read peers now).
- Ettinger-Hoyer: O(log N) copies suffice information-theoretically; post-processing takes exponential time.
- Kuperberg 2003 (SICOMP 2005): time and queries O~(3^{sqrt(2 log_3 N)}), i.e. 2^{sqrt(2 log2 3) sqrt n + o}, for N = 2^n. Quantum space is also 2^{Theta(sqrt n)}. General N gets 2^{O(sqrt n)} with a looser constant.
- Regev 2004: polynomial quantum space, time 2^{O(sqrt(n log n))}.
- Kuperberg 2011 (TQC 2013) collimation sieve: time 2^{O(sqrt n)}, quantum space O(n), classical space 2^{O(sqrt n)}, with tunable trade-offs.
- Peikert (2019, CSIDH analysis) and Bonnetain-Schrottenloher, Bonnetain-Naya-Plasencia: concrete collimation simulations. These are heuristic constants, not full proofs in a bit-operation model.
- No known 2^{o(sqrt n)} algorithm exists. The n^{1/3} goal would be a major breakthrough.

3. Key technical questions.
Q1 (rigour): can a 2^{c sqrt n} bound with explicit c be proved for every N in this exact model? This includes the approximate QFT, Clifford+T synthesis, classical bit costs of sorting/matching labels, and a restart argument for a worst-case (not expected) cutoff. This is achievable but laborious, and it would move the leaderboard index from 1 to 1/2.
Q2 (novelty): is there any arithmetic structure that beats Wagner-style collision exponents? For prime N, the label group is cyclic of prime order. Its only structure is its multiplicative group (Z_p)^*. A multiplicative action on labels (k -> a k) is available classically, but the secret transforms covariantly (s -> a^{-1}...?). Actually w^{(ak)s} = w^{k(as)}, so scaling labels is relabelling, not new information. Multiplying a phase qubit's label by a requires nothing quantum; it is the same state with a reinterpreted secret, which is useless unless combined.
Q3 (robustness): with an eps fraction of malformed copies, collimation trees of 2^{Theta(sqrt n)} leaves amplify corruption. What tolerance can be proved? This is open-ended, explicitly valued by organizers, and plausibly fresh.

Tentative takeaways: the arithmetic lens gives exact reductions (CRT, 2-adic lifting) but leaves prime N as the core. The most valuable novelty may come from Q3 (robust sieving), while Q1 is a mandatory rigorous foundation.
