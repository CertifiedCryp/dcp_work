Round 2 — challenging round-1 assumptions.

A. "Prime N is the same as 2^n with a looser constant." This is false at the level of rigorous analysis, and the reason is arithmetic. Kuperberg's 2-adic sieve for N = 2^n buckets labels by their low b bits, i.e. by cosets of the subgroup 2^b Z_N. Differences of i.i.d. uniform elements of a coset are i.i.d. uniform on the subgroup. So exact uniformity propagates through every level, and the yield analysis is clean. For prime N, Z_N has no nontrivial subgroups. Any magnitude ("archimedean") bucketing uses intervals, which are not cosets, so differences are triangular, not uniform. After a few levels the label law is unknown, and the endgame (hit an exact label) has no clean probability bound. Published general-N analyses either approximate or handle this loosely. This is the real obstruction for a review-grade proof.
Fix I believe works: rejection sampling restores exact uniformity. If two labels are i.i.d. uniform on an integer interval of length W (W even), their difference v has P(v) = (W-|v|)/W^2. Accept v with probability (W/2)/(W-|v|) for v in [-W/2, W/2), else discard. The accepted value is exactly uniform on [-W/2, W/2), the acceptance rate is exactly 1/2, and outcomes on disjoint pairs are independent. Negative labels flip with an exact X gate (|0>+w^{-ks}|1> -> X -> |0>+w^{ks}|1> up to global phase). So an archimedean sieve with per-level loss factor 1/8 (pair, CNOT minus outcome 1/2, acceptance 1/2) keeps labels exactly i.i.d. uniform on centred dyadic intervals. Its pair counts follow from pigeonhole: L labels in B buckets give >= (L-B)/2 pairs deterministically. The yield is therefore distribution-free apart from independent fair coins. Non-dyadic acceptance probabilities need finite-precision coins; truncation adds a TV error that is easy to bound.
Endgame: shrink to the range {-1, 0}; label -1 (prob 1/2) flips to +1. Relabel by a = 2^{-j} mod N (N odd) before sieving. Then a final label 1 in the frame equals original label 2^j, giving the phase qubit for theta_j = frac(2^j s/N). X- and Y-basis Hadamard tests (H, S^dagger: exact Clifford) estimate theta_j to within 1/8 with O(log n) copies. Kitaev's backward reconstruction over j = 0..n+1 recovers s. For the 2-part of N, I'd use the exact coset-state Hensel lift instead: with s_0 known, controlled-subtract s_0 on the |1> branch, measure the parity of the second register, and get an exact coset state over Z_{N/2} for (s-s_0)/2. CRT combines the two parts using s mod 2^e and s mod N_odd from exact reductions x -> x mod m.

B. "The Fourier step is free." It is not. QFT_N is not exactly Clifford+T. I need either a cited theorem (Kitaev / Hales-Hallgren approximate QFT_N with error delta in poly(n, log 1/delta) gates) plus gate synthesis (Solovay-Kitaev / Ross-Selinger), or my own self-contained construction. The hybrid argument is clean: total output TV <= sum of per-sample channel errors. delta = 2^{-(c sqrt n + 10)} costs only poly(n). The reviewer cannot fetch papers, so whatever is cited must have its theorem stated exactly and its applicability argued.

C. "Error tolerance means a probabilistic noise model." The challenge counts malformed copies, not probabilities. Counting argument: if each acquired copy feeds at most one final output tree, then B malformed copies contaminate at most B final outputs. Each contaminated subtree also absorbs at most one good partner per level, so the loss is at most B·d further clean outputs. This holds even if the bad states are adversarial, entangled with each other and with adaptively chosen labels. Clean outputs are exact because selection depends only on classical labels, and conditioned on labels every clean output is the ideal phase qubit. A majority over F outputs survives if F > 2B(d+2). So a sieve whose yield is F/Q tolerates eps of about F/(3dQ).
D. Important constraint from tolerance_expression.py: the fractional-tolerance grammar only accepts min(1, c n^a (log n)^b). An exponentially small tolerance such as 2^{-sqrt n} cannot be declared. A positive declarable tolerance therefore needs 1/poly(n) per-output contamination, i.e. sieve trees with only poly(n) leaves, depth d = a log2 n. Then each level must clear n/d bits, giving time about 2^{n/(a log2 n)}. The structured grammar does support 2^{c n (log n)^{-1}}, since q may be negative rational. This suggests a clean trade-off theorem: T = 2^{Theta(n / log(1/eps))}, which interpolates between Kuperberg (eps = 2^{-sqrt n}) and constant tolerance with 2^{n/d}. At index 1 this would still be a strictly better full bound than exhaustive search, with a declared positive inverse-polynomial tolerance.

Open worries: (i) Does any algorithm achieve 2^{o(n)} with constant-fraction tolerance? Outputs that depend on a growing number of copies seem doomed under random corruption, unless combination is itself error-correcting. (ii) The rejection-sampling uniformity argument must be checked carefully with integer endpoints. (iii) Sample cost of the relabel-per-j approach: n+2 separate sieves, each with O(log n) outputs, multiply cost by poly(n). That is fine.
